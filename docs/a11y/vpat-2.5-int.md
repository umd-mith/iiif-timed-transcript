## Terms

| Level                  | Definition                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Supports**           | The functionality of the product has at least one method that meets the criterion without known defects, or meets with equivalent facilitation. |
| **Partially Supports** | Some functionality of the product does not meet the criterion.                                                                                  |
| **Does Not Support**   | The majority of product functionality does not meet the criterion.                                                                              |
| **Not Applicable**     | The criterion is not relevant to the product.                                                                                                   |
| **Not Evaluated**      | The product has not been evaluated against the criterion. Under VPAT 2.5 this level may be used **only** for WCAG Level AAA criteria.           |

### Declared deviation: "Not Evaluated" at Level A and AA

**This report uses "Not Evaluated" on five Level A and AA criteria, which
VPAT 2.5 does not permit.** The rows are **1.3.4**, **1.4.4**, **1.4.12**,
**2.5.2**, and **4.1.2**. This is a conscious deviation, declared here rather
than buried, and a reviewer is entitled to reject it.

The reasoning: the permitted alternative is to score one of the four real
levels, each of which asserts something about the product we have not
established. At 4.1.2 the open question is whether NVDA and VoiceOver expose
an open shadow root's accessibility tree correctly — historically where those
two diverge — and no amount of source inspection answers it. To record
"Supports" there would be the exact failure this exercise exists to avoid; to
record "Does Not Support" would be equally unfounded. We would rather deviate
visibly from the template than comply with it by guessing.

If an independent reviewer judges that the format must be respected, the
remedy is to run the outstanding passes and score the rows properly, not to
relabel them. The passes are written and committed. See Evidence.

## Component, not page

WCAG conformance is defined for a full page. This report describes
`<iiif-transcript-player>` evaluated in isolation on a minimal host page. Any
page that embeds it depends also on that page and on the IIIF manifest it
supplies. Three categories of criteria are marked accordingly:

1. **Host-page criteria** the component cannot satisfy on its own — page
   language, bypass blocks, page title, consistent navigation and
   identification across pages — marked **Not Applicable**. The embedding
   page owns them.
2. **Content-dependent criteria**, the 1.2.x block. See the Content
   Responsibility note above that block.
3. **Theming-dependent criteria**, 1.4.3 and 1.4.11. Those claims hold for
   the shipped default `--iiif-player-*` values only. See "Theming
   responsibility" in `README.md`.

**A note on the host pages used for testing.**
`examples/element/plain.html` appears in the test scripts, but its style
block is deliberately hostile: it remaps `--iiif-player-accent`, among other
things, to prove that tokens do not leak. It is the right page for keyboard
and screen-reader work and the **wrong** page for any claim scoped to shipped
defaults. Default-appearance claims below rest on
`docs/src/pages/canvas-nav-demo.astro`, which applies no `::part()` overrides
and no token remapping.

## Content Responsibility note (applies to the whole 1.2.x block)

The component renders whatever caption tracks and transcript annotations the
supplied IIIF manifest carries. It neither generates nor validates captions,
transcripts, or audio descriptions. **Conformance with 1.2.1 through 1.2.5
for any embedded item depends on the manifest publisher's content**, not on
this component. Where a row below reflects a gap in the _component's own
behavior_ rather than in manifest content, the Remarks say so and distinguish
the two.

The component does one piece of good citizenship here: a development-time
console warning, numbered by success criterion, when a video canvas has no
caption tracks at all (`src/lib/player/Viewer.svelte` — "[IIIFPlayer] Video
has no caption tracks… for WCAG 1.2.2 compliance."). Cited as a mitigation,
not as a substitute for any row's conformance level.

| **4.1.2** Name, Role, Value                                    | A     | **Not Evaluated** _(declared deviation)_     | Pending the NVDA with Firefox and VoiceOver with Safari passes in `screen-reader-scripts.md`, which have no recorded results. Source-level roles, names, and states are all present — see 1.3.1, plus `aria-current`, `aria-busy` (`Transcript.svelte:341`), and `aria-valuetext` on Progress (`Progress.svelte:35`). The open question is not what the DOM says but whether assistive technology exposes an open shadow root's accessibility tree correctly, and nothing short of a screen reader answers it. This report does not claim the row on source inspection.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **4.1.3** Status Messages                                      | AA    | **Partially Supports**                       | Two `aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true"` regions exist: one in the transcript panel (`Transcript.svelte:374`) and the search match counter (`Search.svelte:121`). The panel's region is **a single node fed by a single `announcementText` value** that two effects write to: the active-segment announcer (`Transcript.svelte:281`), gated on user-initiated interaction so that it stays silent as playback auto-advances, and the transcript-status announcer (`:299`, `:301`), which carries "Transcript loaded, N segments" and "Transcript unavailable". The region is now mounted persistently rather than alongside its content, which closes the case where a failed transcript was silent to assistive technology. The row is Partially Supports rather than Supports for two reasons: that shared-container overwrite, and the fact that whether any of it is spoken at all is checks 4 to 8 of `screen-reader-scripts.md`, which have not been run. |
